About

One person is accountable for your build. That person is me.

I'm Adam Johnston. I own Utah Golf Sim, I run the consultation, I coordinate the install, and I'm the number you call if something isn't right.

I build golf simulators because I got hooked on the game this year and immediately hit the wall every new golfer hits: the range closes, the weather turns, and the time you actually have to practice never lines up with when the course is open. A simulator fixes all of it. Turns out the hard part isn't the golf — it's building the room right. That's my part.

Before this, I spent six years in contracting. That's where I learned what actually decides whether a job goes well: show up when you said you would, keep the site clean, and tell people the truth about what their space can do.

I was the most expensive guy in my market, and I told clients that to their face. They hired me anyway — because they'd rather pay more once than pay less twice.

Here's what most people don't see coming: a golf simulator is a construction project wearing a technology costume. Framing, sightlines, power, acoustics, and then a launch monitor calibrated until the numbers on the screen match the ball in the air. I've watched people get sold these by outfits that had never framed a wall — and by single-brand installers who recommend whatever they happen to sell.

That's the whole reason Utah Golf Sim exists. I build with Uneekor, TruGolf, and Carl's Place — not just one of them — so I can spec your room against all four and tell you which one actually fits: your ceiling, your light, your budget, and the way you really play. Not which one pays me best.

I started this in July 2026, which makes you an early client, and I'm not going to dress that up. Here's the trade: an insured Utah business, six years of real contracting experience behind every framing and calibration call, and a golfer who wants his first builds to be the ones people can't stop talking about — because those builds are the whole business.

Where we work

Provo to Park City.

We cover four counties across the Wasatch Front and Back. In Utah County and Salt Lake County that means Provo, Orem, Springville, Lehi, American Fork, Draper, Sandy, South Jordan, Herriman, Holladay, and Salt Lake City itself.

Over the mountain, Summit County and Wasatch County are where indoor golf earns its keep: Park City, Kimball Junction, Promontory, Midway, and Heber City. Courses up there close for a good chunk of the year, ski houses tend to have the ceiling height a full swing actually needs, and a simulator room is the difference between a six-month golf season and a twelve-month one.

Just outside those lines? Ask. For the right project we'll make the drive.
